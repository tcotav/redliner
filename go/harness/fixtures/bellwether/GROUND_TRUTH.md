# Ground Truth

## Contradictions

### 1. Length of Ren's absence
- **Sections:** 1 and 3
- **Section 1:** "Nineteen winters had passed since Renata Sowa last stood on the Bellwether town dock, and the dock had not improved."
- **Section 3:** "Twenty-three years. That was the arithmetic she kept doing at two in the morning: she had been away twenty-three years, long enough that the girl at the marina counter who sold her bronze ring nails had not been born when Ren left town."
- **Why incompatible:** A single continuous absence cannot be both nineteen years and twenty-three years long; both are given as narrated fact, not as a character's estimate.
- **Wording variation:** "Nineteen winters" (seasonal idiom, full name "Renata Sowa") versus "twenty-three years" (plain arithmetic, "Ren").

### 2. Age of the father at death
- **Sections:** 1 and 3
- **Section 1:** "Emil had died in March, eighty-one years old, in the hospice wing of the county hospital."
- **Section 3:** "And her father had died two months short of his seventy-seventh birthday, which meant the number of years she had known him and the number of years she had avoided him were closer to even than she could stand to think about."
- **Why incompatible:** He was either eighty-one or seventy-six at death; the two ages differ by five years and both are stated flatly by the narration.
- **Wording variation:** Named as "Emil" with a direct age ("eighty-one years old") in one; called "her father" with an indirect construction ("two months short of his seventy-seventh birthday") in the other.

### 3. Length of the Lyman
- **Sections:** 1 and 4
- **Section 1:** "A 1962 hull, twenty-six feet of white oak and mahogany, half of it planked and half of it open ribs, exactly as her father had left it."
- **Section 4:** "Thirty-one feet of white oak and mahogany came off the slings and sat down on the water and did what old wooden boats do, which is leak, generously and without apology, until the planks took up."
- **Why incompatible:** The same hull is measured at twenty-six feet and at thirty-one feet; a boat has one length.
- **Wording variation:** Named as "the Lyman / A 1962 hull" in one; referred to only by its measurement and materials at the launch in the other, with the boat's name never repeated.

### 4. Whether anyone was with the father when he died
- **Sections:** 2 and 4
- **Section 2:** "Her sister had sat with him all four of those days, and had held his hand through the last four hours of it, and had sung him that stupid song about the pierogi while the machines counted him down."
- **Section 4:** "Neither of them had been in the room at the end. The hospice nurse had phoned Kaja at six in the morning on the fifth of March to say that Emil had gone in the night, alone, sometime after the two o'clock check, and Kaja had driven the eleven miles in the dark and sat in the parking lot for forty minutes before she could make herself go in."
- **Why incompatible:** Kaja cannot have held his hand through his final four hours and also have been away, phoned at six a.m., while he died alone sometime after a two o'clock check. Section 4's narration states flatly that neither daughter was present.
- **Wording variation:** First half is narration through Ren's view, referring to "her sister" and carrying sensory detail (the hand, the song); second half names "Kaja" and "Emil" and reports the night through clock times, a phone call, and the hospice nurse.

## Decoys

### A. Two boats
- **Sections:** 1/3 (Lyman) and 3 (duck skiff)
- **Section 1:** "And there, on blocks in the center bay under a canvas tarp gone stiff with dust, was his boat, the Lyman."
- **Section 3:** "His boat sat behind the shop under a tarp of its own, the flat-bottomed duck skiff he had built in one weekend in 1988 out of marine ply and spite, after a man in Hammond Bay told him a boat like that couldn't be built in a weekend."
- **Not a contradiction:** "His boat, under a tarp" appears in two incompatible places, but Emil owned more than one boat; the Lyman on blocks in the center bay and the duck skiff behind the shop are two different hulls, each named in its own sentence.

### B. The harbor's water level
- **Sections:** 2 and 4
- **Section 2:** "You could walk out forty feet past the end of the dock and not wet your knees."
- **Section 4:** "That morning the water stood over the dock boards at the outer end, so that the last twenty feet of pier looked like a path laid across the surface, and men in waders swore cheerfully at the travel lift."
- **Not a contradiction:** The two descriptions are explicitly separated in time — an August low-water summer roughly twenty-five years earlier versus the morning of 14 November after a fall of rising lake levels. Great Lakes water levels move over years; both can be true.

### C. Kaja's claim that their father never asked about Ren
- **Sections:** 2 and 4
- **Section 2:** "He never once asked about you," Kaja said. "Not once, Ren. Every Christmas I sat across from him and he never said your name."
- **Section 4:** "Then she went below and came back with the shop ledger, open to the inside cover, where their father had written her Portland address in pencil and crossed it out and written it again, three times, three apartments, the last one current as of a year ago."
- **Not a contradiction:** Kaja's line is dialogue about what Emil said in her presence, which the narration never confirms or denies; a man can keep a daughter's current address without ever speaking her name at Christmas dinner.
