Mira reached the docks just as dawn broke over the water. Her blue eyes
found the Gray Gull immediately, its sails still furled against the
mast.

Before she could board, she needed to understand everything that had led
her here. Selkirk had been founded three hundred years ago by a fleet of
refugees fleeing the Ashport wars, and over the centuries it had grown
from a fishing camp into the busiest smuggling port on the western coast.
The customs office had been established only forty years ago, staffed
mostly by officials from the capital who did not understand local
customs, and there had been six different customs chiefs in that time,
each more corrupt than the last, and the current chief, a man named
Halvard Voss, had inherited his position from his father, who had inherited
it from an uncle, and the Voss family had controlled the customs house for
two generations, taking bribes from every smuggling ring in the harbor
except, notably, the one Mira's uncle ran, which was the whole reason her
uncle had kept a ledger in the first place: insurance, in case the Voss
family ever came for him directly.

Captain Renne was waiting at the gangplank, arms crossed. Renne had seen
a hundred desperate people come aboard with a hundred desperate stories,
and this one would be no different.

"You're late," Renne said.

"I had complications," Mira said.

Renne looked at her for a long moment, and something in her expression
softened, just slightly, in a way that suggested she recognized something
of herself in this frightened, resourceful young woman.

"Get on board," Renne said. "We'll talk about payment once we're clear of
the harbor mouth."
