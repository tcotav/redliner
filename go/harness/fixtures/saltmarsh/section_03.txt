He took it to Ilse Marrow, because there was no one else, and because
she had told him twenty years ago never to bring her anything again.

Her house above the saltmarsh smelled of tar and cold tea. She looked at
the instrument for a long time without touching it, and her brown eyes
did not blink as much as they should have.

"You've had this three weeks," she said.

"Two."

"You've had it three weeks, Ferris, because that's how long it takes to
get to the part where you bring it to me." She sat back. "I'm not going
to tell you to destroy it. You wouldn't, and then you'd have to lie
about that too."

He thought of the tide clock on his own wall, stopped these fifteen
years, and how he wound it anyway.

"Tell me what it counts."

"You already know what it counts."

He did. He had known since the tenth day. Saying it aloud in her kitchen
would make it a thing that two people knew, and he was not ready, and he
understood — with a clarity that felt like the cold coming along the
floor of his shop — that he was never going to be ready, and that this
was precisely what the woman with the grey eyes had been counting on.

Outside, the tide went out over the saltmarsh, and did not come back at
the hour it should have.
