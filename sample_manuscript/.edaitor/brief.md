## What it is

- **Logline:** A smuggler's niece steals the ledger that names every
  smuggler in her harbor town, and has to buy passage out before the
  customs office — or her uncle — catches up with her.
- **Genre / subgenre:** YA adventure, secondary-world, low fantasy
- **Audience:** 14+, readers of nautical adventure and heist stories
- **Comps:** *Six of Crows*, *The Girl of Ink & Stars*
- **Target length:** 80,000 words

## Draft stage

**Stage:** first complete draft

Severity implication for this stage: developmental focus. Line findings
only if `major` or above, or if they identify a manuscript-wide pattern
rather than a local slip.

## Voice and structure

- **POV:** Tight third person, Mira only
- **Tense:** Past
- **Structure:** Linear, chronological

## Deliberate choices — do NOT report these as errors

- **Renne is never given interiority.** She's deliberately opaque for the
  first act — the reader should be as uncertain about her motives as Mira
  is. Distance from Renne is the design, not a failure to develop her.
- **Chapter 1 opens mid-chase**, with no orienting establishment of the
  town or Mira's situation. The disorientation is intended; context
  arrives afterward.
- **Short, clipped sentences during action.** Fragments are fine in
  chase and fight scenes.

## Known problem areas

- Chapter 2's opening exposition is too heavy. The author knows and is
  looking for guidance on where the material should go, not to be told
  it's a problem.
- The ending is unwritten; these two chapters are the opening only.

## Off-limits

- The harbor-town setting and the ledger MacGuffin are fixed. Not
  interested in "what if it were a different object."

## What the author wants from this pass

- Does the opening create enough forward pressure to carry a reader into
  chapter 3?
- Is Mira's motivation legible before the ledger's stakes are explained?
- Where should chapter 2's history material actually go?
